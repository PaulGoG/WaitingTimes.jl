References

The waiting time with a threshold is the investment horizon of Simonsen et al. [6], the shortest time in which the return of an asset reaches a given level, which Vivirschi et al. [7] adapted to earthquake magnitudes. Pană et al. [8] applied it to the sea level of Trieste and defined the detrending and pruning of the preprocessed series; Gogîță et al. [1] followed the distributions across thresholds in weather, sea-level, currency and automotive data.

The exact-law tests (Validation) take their expected values from the first-passage theory of random walks: the survival probability $\binom{2k}{k} 4^{-k}$ at $\delta = 0$ for continuous symmetric steps [4] and $P(\tau = k) = (a/k)\, P(S_k = a)$ for the simple walk at level $a$ [5].

SegmentTreeSearch uses the segment tree [9, 10] and FenwickSweep the binary indexed tree [11]. At $\delta = 0$ the passage of an index is its nearest larger-or-equal value to the right, the all-nearest-larger-values problem [12], found with a monotone stack; StreamingSearch generalises the stack to $\delta > 0$ with a priority queue.

The automatic choice of digits rests on what rounding to a grid does to a distribution [2, 13] and on the grid step measured against the dispersion of the quantity [3].
